# Portfolio design

The page presents Shawn's engineering work with a small guitar influence. It uses the UI Skills Swiss-design guidance and Animate timing/accessibility guidance: clear hierarchy, an ordered grid, generous space, and brief purposeful movement.

## Layout and typography

- A 12-column grid within a 1280px page, with 24px mobile and 48px desktop gutters.
- IBM Plex Sans, with system sans-serif fallback. Lightweight, oversized name typography anchors the introduction; small monospaced labels provide supporting context.
- The desktop introduction pairs the name, Waterloo label, and a row of GitHub, LinkedIn, email, and resume icons with a separate guitar area. There are no introductory marketing paragraphs or header monogram. The opening occupies at least the first viewport, with the Software / Hardware / Sound strip, Easter-egg link, and GitHub text link below it. The former Explore my work and Get in touch text links are removed. On narrow screens, the guitar follows the name. The Resume link uses unaccented spelling and a lavender outline to stand out from blue interaction accents.
- Projects, experience, and about use section labels on the left and content on the right, collapsing to one column on mobile.
- Projects, Experience, and About each sit in a bordered dark panel with a short accent rule and generous separation. Section labels use 16px type on mobile and 18px on desktop.
- Each project has its own outlined inset panel. Experience separates the current role from its work; About separates engineering from life outside it. Native expandable build notes preserve keyboard and no-JavaScript behavior.
- The underwater background uses a few broad surface ripples, soft descending light shafts, and four current contours. Scrolling moves these layers upward at different speeds and fades the scene into deep navy. Avoid dots, particle fields, and busy textures. Panels have translucent blue edges and dark inset cards to keep content readable.
- A “Below the surface / Scroll to dive” link separates the introduction from the work. A quiet desktop depth gauge progresses from 000 to 120 m; these numbers are a visual metaphor for page progress. It hides below 1480px so it never crowds the content.
- Faceted blue-slate cliff silhouettes emerge along the viewport edges at different depths. Near the bottom, a layered seabed, a few stones, and sparse sea grass complete the descent. Keep the central reading area open. These decorative layers use native CSS scroll timelines; reduced motion removes their parallax, and narrow layouts tuck them farther into the edges.
- A small chocolate Labrador walks across the seabed once per visit to the bottom tenth of the page. The gait pairs opposite legs, a slight body bounce, head nod, and tail wag. Walking pauses when leaving the bottom area or hiding the tab; scrolling above 80% resets the visit. Reduced motion shows a stationary dog. Extra space beneath the footer keeps its route clear of links and buttons.

## Palette and guitar rig

The site stays dark: `#0b0e14` background, `#e6edf7` text, and `#91caff` accents. Muted text and separators derive from the ink. Keep the rock influence restrained: line-drawn hardware, a small amp, one cool accent, and a faint amp glow when connected.

There is no header theme switch or acoustic guitar. The electric guitar starts unplugged on each load and plays a quiet clean tone. A draggable quarter-inch cable plug sits beside an amp input. Releasing its tip near the jack connects it, turns on the amp indicator, and enables distortion. Pulling it away restores clean tone. The flexible cord and visible plug share one SVG coordinate system, so their junction stays intact through scrolling, scaling, and resizing. A separate transparent button supplies pointer capture and keyboard focus. The cord follows the grip within the illustration area and stays anchored to the guitar.

When connected, the plug's collar sits against the jack and the metal shaft is concealed inside the socket. Soft cream and pale-blue sunlight fans out from above, widening and fading through the water. Six feathered beams drift over 22 seconds with a gentle 11–16-second shimmer, without neon cores or traveling streaks. The amp retains its purple-blue grille glow and blue indicator. Its surrounding halo fades fully to transparent at the layer edges, with a 600ms opacity transition and no sharp grille highlight. Only transforms and opacity animate. Unplugging fades the sunlight over 600ms and pauses the beams; hidden tabs pause them too. Reduced motion retains static light. All lighting stays behind content and never captures input.

## Motion and interaction

Hover motion lasts about 150ms; guitar artwork transitions last 180–240ms. The underwater sequence intentionally uses larger, longer movements: arrivals take roughly one second, and the waterline follows the user's scroll. Transitions animate opacity and transforms. Guitar strings briefly vibrate on activation, without forced layout reads. Reduced-motion preferences disable nonessential animation.

Offscreen sections rise 170px from a smaller, tilted perspective. Cards enter from alternating sides, rotating and scaling into place. The transform settles over 1100ms and opacity over 650ms, with content blocks staggered by 65ms. Each reveal runs once. Content is visible by default without JavaScript; reduced motion reveals everything immediately, and keyboard focus reveals pending ancestors immediately without a transition.

The ocean follows native scrolling without scroll interception. A full-width curved waterline sweeps upward between the intro and projects while the intro recedes. Native CSS scroll timelines move whole layers through transforms and opacity. Current contours translate together on one composited plane without scaling, rotation, or individual SVG path updates, keeping thin strokes steady while scrolling. Keyboard focus holds the intro still. JavaScript only refreshes the depth label at most every 80ms and changes discrete scene and dog states; it does not drive scroll animation frames. Timeline ranges are measured together after viewport or content-height changes. The depth gauge is isolated so its changing numbers do not repaint the page. Reduced motion and browsers without scroll timelines use static depth states, retaining the gauge and readable content. Core content stays available without JavaScript. The cursor glow and amplifier lights remain independent of ocean depth.

The name rises and rotates into place over 1000–1100ms, with a 120ms stagger between lines. Strumming adds body recoil, small resonance rings, and two large sonar ripples that expand from the guitar across the viewport over 3200ms when unplugged (quiet clean tone) or 1600ms when plugged in (distortion). The second ripple follows after 360ms clean or 180ms driven. Each ripple uses three asymmetric SVG contours, with independent expansion, drift, rotation, and gentle stretching. Contours vary per strum, clean waves are blue, and driven waves alternate purple and blue with contrasting contour highlights. Repeated strums overlap up to six crests; completed crests are removed. Blur, tab hiding, page exit, and reduced motion clear all active crests. Paths are generated once per crest and only transforms and opacity animate. Hovering only animates the strings. Cable dragging uses pointer capture and one scheduled render per frame; geometry is measured at gesture start and resize, not in a perpetual animation loop. Reduced motion keeps direct dragging available while disabling decorative motion.

Mouse and touch share the same drag behavior. The plug and input also support tap and keyboard activation. Escape, pointer cancellation, and lost capture restore the previous position and connection. Focus indicators and live connection announcements provide feedback. Responsive resizing keeps an inserted plug docked.

Use the native cursor, with grab/grabbing feedback on the cable plug. A soft blue halo follows mouse movement without a pick graphic, idles when settled, and never intercepts input. It hides on touch and keyboard navigation and follows directly with reduced motion. Small discoveries remain in the name's period, project drawings, four-strum message, `encore` shortcut, and footer hints. Sound is enabled by default and starts on a guitar click or tap, with a visible mute toggle. Plugging in routes the electric voice through a high-gain preamp, oversampled distortion, and cabinet filtering; unplugging bypasses that chain and lowers the volume.

Keep focus indicators visible and controls comfortably tappable. Core content must remain readable without JavaScript. Preserve the existing project names, links, technical details, and experience; Allos is the current project name.

Easter eggs are discoverable through an intro link, a visible native disclosure beneath About, and a labeled footer button. The guide offers direct actions for the musical notes, guitar, and encore palette, with text explaining each interaction. Keep the hidden shortcuts working alongside these controls.

## Development feedback

Agentation mounts only in development, using an isolated React root. Manual copy/paste mode has no server endpoint. Production remains a static HTML/CSS/JavaScript site without the toolbar or React runtime.
